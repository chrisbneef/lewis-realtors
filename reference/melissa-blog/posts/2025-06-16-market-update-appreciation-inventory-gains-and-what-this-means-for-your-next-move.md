## Portland, OR Metro Market Trends – April 2025

We're seeing a subtle but critical shift in the Portland, OR housing market that you should know about:

- New listings are up to 3,358, a noticeable increase from 2024
- Pending sales dipped slightly to 2,225
- Closed sales remained steady at 1,880 compared to last year (only three down!)
- Average home price climbed to $616,700 (vs. ~$597,000 in March)
- Inventory is up to 3.1 months (compared to 2.4 last year)
- Days on market also increased to 62 (up from 55)
What stands out? Prices continue to rise even as the market time grows. This tells me we're sitting in a neutral market—a rare balance of power between buyers and sellers.

As I always promise, "I’m here to walk you through the data so you can make the sound decisions that are right for your family and your goals."

## Mid‑Willamette Area (Polk & Marion Counties)

Next, let’s head south to the Mid-Willamette area, where the story continues in stride:

- New listings decreased slightly to 336
- Pending sales are steady at 235
- Closed sales dropped a bit to 168
- Average sale price rose to $475,000
- Median price also climbed to ~$450,000
- Inventory at 3.5 months, up from last year
- Days on market at 57, consistent with 2024
We’re continuing to see appreciation here. Sellers, if you’ve been wondering whether your home value is holding up, it’s not just holding, it’s climbing.

## Southwest Washington: Vancouver, WA Real Estate Snapshot

Over the bridge into Vancouver, WA and Clark County, the market is following the same upward trend—with a side of no income tax, which empty nesters love.

Here’s what we’re seeing:

- New listings up at 889 for April
- Pending sales slightly down to 565
- Closed sales inched up to 535
- Average price increased to $644,000
- Median price rose to about $555,000
- More inventory and longer days on market
This paints a picture of a thoughtful market.

Buyers are taking their time, making sure the home checks all the boxes. Sellers, don’t panic if your home takes a bit longer to sell. "There’s just longer thinking time, and it's okay."
