# Thinking of Holding an Open House? Here's What the Latest Buyer Data Tells Us

If you’re a homeowner in Portland, OR, Vancouver, WA, or the Mid-Willamette Valley preparing to sell your $1M+ home, you’ve probably wondered: Do open houses really work anymore? This is one of the most common questions I get from my clients, and the answer may surprise you.

We’ve all seen homes advertised on real estate platforms with “Open House this weekend!” and it's natural to assume it’s a necessary step in the process. But in today’s market, especially in well-loved communities like ours where home selling decisions are thoughtfully made, it’s critical to dig into the data and think through what really aligns with your goals.

## The Truth About Open Houses: What the Numbers Show

Homeowners, especially those in premier neighborhoods with strong community ties, want to make sure every step of their home selling journey is intentional and effective. That’s why I like to empower you with the data behind the strategy.

According to the National Association of Realtors 2024 Profile of Buyers and Sellers, only 3% to 5% of buyers actually purchased a home that they first toured during an open house. That means the chances your ideal buyer will walk in off the street and submit an offer is statistically very low.

On the other hand:

- 51% of buyers who submitted offers initially found the home online
- 29% found the home through their real estate agent
If you’re aiming to attract serious, qualified buyers, it’s clear where the focus should be: online presence and agent-driven exposure.

## The Pros and Cons of Hosting an Open House

Of course, open houses aren’t completely off the table. There are pros and cons to consider, especially if you’re juggling a busy schedule, thinking about downsizing, or preparing for a seasonal move out of Portland, OR, Vancouver, WA, or the Mid-Willamette Valley.

???? PROS:

Creates visibility and neighborhood buzz

- Neighbors may spread the word, which can amplify exposure
Gives you a predictable window of showings

- Especially helpful if you prefer to step away from the house for a few hours and avoid staggered private showings
Allows potential buyers to casually browse

- This could appeal to those just starting the exploration stage
???? CONS:

Low conversion to actual offers

- “Open houses rarely trigger the offer,” and buyer urgency typically isn't cultivated at open events
Security risk (especially for vacant properties)

- People coming through are unverified, and unfortunately, some may just be casing the house
Requires frequent and consistent cleaning

- Before each open house, your home needs to be spotless, which can be challenging with a busy family life
High volume of non-serious visitors

- “They are notoriously known for looky-loos, neighbors, and nonqualified buyers”
With today's longer days on the market, you’re putting a lot of hopes into a single window of time, betting that your ideal buyer is available and motivated to take action that day.
