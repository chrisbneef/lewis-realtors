# Did Home Prices Drop in Portland, OR, Vancouver, WA, and the Mid-Willamette Valley This January?

January's real estate report for Portland, OR, Vancouver, WA, and the Mid-Willamette Valley is here, and it offers a glimpse into the emerging trends for 2026. These early-year numbers are essential as they set the tone for the rest of the year. Let's explore what these numbers mean for both buyers and sellers in these vibrant areas.

## January's Real Estate Overview

A surge in listings has been a highlight for the Portland, OR metro area, with listings up by 109% from December to January. This increase is notable considering the holiday season, indicating more than just a seasonal trend.

Other key highlights include:

- Pending sales increased by 38.4%, showcasing steady demand
- Median sales price dropped by nearly 5% from December, settling at $510,000
- Average sales price mirrored this decrease, down to $568,000
Inventory levels saw significant growth, with 4.3 months of inventory available. These numbers set the stage for what could be a dynamic Spring market.

## Real Estate Trends in Polk and Marion Counties

Further south, the Polk and Marion counties show similar patterns to Portland's market trends:

- New listings jumped 56.7% from December
- Year-over-year, new listings increased by 16.3%
- The median price decreased by 5.2% to $426,500
With 5.9 months of inventory, these areas demonstrate consistent consumer confidence and market stability.

## Clark County’s Insights

The Clark County area in Southwest Washington also displays these trends:

- New listings doubled with 704 added in January
- Pending sales rose nearly 50%
- Inventory increased to 4.1 months, hinting at an active Spring market
The market here continues to be stable with median and average sales prices maintaining their levels from December.

## Reflections on Current Market Conditions

In my experiences with clients, I've noticed a shift towards more deliberate decision-making among buyers. However, an increase in market interest is evident:

- Homes in the mid-$500,000 range are seeing some competitive interest
- Properties closer to the $1 million mark, such as condos, have slightly less competition
