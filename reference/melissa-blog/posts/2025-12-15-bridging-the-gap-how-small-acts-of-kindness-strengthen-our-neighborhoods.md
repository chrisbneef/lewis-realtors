# Bridging the Gap: How Small Acts of Kindness Strengthen Our Neighborhoods

The holidays are meant to be a time of warmth, connection, and community, but for many, it’s the most isolating time of year. As someone deeply invested in both personal and community well-being, I’ve been reflecting on how we can truly be there for one another.

Recently, I found myself visiting an old neighbor who had just lost her husband. It was emotional and heartfelt, especially considering that eight years ago, she stood at my doorstep with a blanket in hand shortly after I had gone through two brain surgeries. Now, I was returning that act of kindness with one of our Lewis Love blankets; full circle, wrapped in care.

That visit stirred more than just memories, it raised a powerful question: How many of our neighbors are silently struggling with loneliness, especially during the holiday season?

## Understanding the Impact of Loneliness During the Holidays

According to the American Psychological Association, over one-third of adults in the U.S. feel lonely every week. And even more striking, 10% report feeling lonely every single day.

A recent Value Penguin survey showed that 61% feel lonely during the holidays. That’s more than half of us dealing with emotional isolation at a time when joy is supposed to be abundant.

Living in beautiful neighborhoods throughout Portland, OR, Vancouver, WA, and the Mid-Willamette Valley, it’s easy to assume everyone is thriving, but the reality is that many people, especially older adults and empty nesters may be quietly navigating significant grief, loss, or transition.

## Why So Many Feel Lonely

Loneliness isn’t just about physical isolation. It’s often rooted in:

- Loss of a spouse or family member
- Children moving away across the country or abroad
- Friends relocating or aging out of activity
- Seasonal blues and lack of sunlight, especially in the Pacific Northwest
- Retirement or lifestyle shifts that reduce everyday interactions
Loneliness happens silently, even in the most vibrant communities.

## Simple Ways to Support Neighbors During the Holidays

We all desire connection, and sometimes the smallest gesture can be the greatest gift. Here are some thoughtful and simple ways you can check in and uplift your neighbors this season.

- Extend No-Pressure Invitations
Some people may feel hesitant to reach out or say yes because they don’t want to feel like a burden. That’s where soft, no-pressure connections become powerful.

Try:

- “Would you like to grab coffee this week?”
- “Hey, I’m running to the store, want to join?”
- “I’m taking a winter walk later, want to come with me?”
These small ways of saying I see you, and I want you near can matter so much more than we realize.

- Ask for Their Input
Sometimes people just want to feel needed and valuable. One of my favorite ways to help someone feel seen and included is to ask for their advice or opinion.

Things like:

- “I’d value your opinion on something. Can I run something by you?”
- “Would you mind if I came over and we wrapped presents together?”
- “Do you want to watch a holiday movie together this weekend?”
This shifts the energy from “you need help” to “you’re an important part of my life.”

- Identify One Person You Can Check In On
Pause for a moment and think: Who do I know who may be feeling alone right now? Maybe it’s an empty nester. Maybe it’s a friend who lost a spouse or whose children live far away. Maybe it’s someone who just moved to Portland, OR, Vancouver, WA, or the Mid-Willamette Valley and hasn’t developed their social circle yet.

Even a simple text or call like “Hey, how are you doing?” opens the door.
