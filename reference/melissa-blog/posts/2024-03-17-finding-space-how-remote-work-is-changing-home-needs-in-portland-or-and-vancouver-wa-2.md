# Finding Space: How Remote Work is Changing Home Needs in Portland, OR and Vancouver, WA

## Adapting to Remote Work Home Trends

## In the unpredictable changes in today s work culture, many of us find ourselves re-evaluating our living spaces to better fit our new remote work lifestyles. As I've navigated these changes both personally and professionally, I've gathered insights that might help you do the same.

## The Rise of Remote Work

It s undeniable that remote work has dramatically altered our daily routines. Pre-pandemic, only about 5-7% of people worked from home, but recent statistics suggest that we re looking at around 22-25% going forward into 2025. This shift isn't just temporary; it s a fundamental change in how we view work and home life.

## The Impact on Home Space Needs

## As someone very familiar with the real estate landscapes of Portland, OR, and Vancouver, WA, I ve seen first-hand how these remote work trends are affecting home buying and selling:

- Increased Demand for Space: Many of you are probably navigating your home that is now suddenly doubling as an office. About 13% of homeowners are in the same boat, looking to expand their living areas to accommodate work-from-home needs.
- Value of Dedicated Workspaces: Homes with dedicated office spaces or adaptable layouts are more appealing than ever. The need for a clear boundary between work and personal life is crucial for maintaining a healthy work-life balance.

## Tips for Embracing Remote Work

While waiting to make your next move, whether it's upgrading to a larger home or simply reorganizing your current space, here are a few strategies to enhance your remote work experience:

- Designate Work Boundaries: Establish clear boundaries for where and when work happens. This helps in maintaining a routine and mentally separating work hours from personal time.
- Stay Connected: Utilize community resources to stay connected. Engage in local events, join a hiking club, or participate in community potlucks. Local Facebook events and church activities can also be great resources.
- Take Regular Breaks: Just stepping out for a 15-minute walk or getting some fresh air can dramatically increase your productivity and mental health.
