# Thinking About Solar Panels? Here’s What Nobody Tells You Before Selling a Home

If you’ve been thinking about installing solar panels on your home, it’s likely because you’re excited about several benefits: lower utility bills, reducing your carbon footprint, and investing in green energy. Those are all great reasons to go solar. But here’s the thing many homeowners in the Portland, OR, Vancouver, WA, and the Mid-Willamette Valley areas don’t realize, if you’re also considering selling your home now or in the future, solar panels can cause unexpected issues and may impact your bottom line.

I’m Melissa Shaw, your trusted real estate advisor, and having helped numerous clients navigate the selling process with solar panels involved, there are a few essential pros and cons you should understand before moving forward.

## The Upside of Going Solar

Let’s start with the positives. There are several compelling reasons why homeowners like you consider solar panels:

- Reduces your energy bills
- Supports sustainability and eco-conscious living
- Modernizes your home with an eco-forward update
- Attractive feature for buyers who value energy-efficient living
If you're planning to stay long-term in your home and pay for the system upfront, going solar might be a fantastic fit for you. But it’s a different story if you think there’s even a chance you might want to sell sometime down the road.

## What Most Homeowners Don’t Realize About Solar Panels and Home Sales

Here’s where it gets more complex. If you’re going solar and plan to finance the system through a lease or a loan, there are several challenges you need to be aware of. And these are issues I’ve seen play out in real-time with my clients.

One of my sellers had financed solar panels and didn’t loop me in beforehand. It ended up taking over 60 hours of additional work to sort through all the barriers to closing. That’s work I’m always willing to do for my clients, but ideally, with the right information up front, we can avoid that altogether.

## Key Problems to Watch Out For

Here are the biggest issues I’ve seen homeowners face with financed solar panels before listing a home:

- Liens Must Be Released Before Selling
Even if solar panels are marketed to you as “personal property,” financing companies often place a lien on your home until the loan is paid off. That does affect the sale. Most of the time, you’ll need a lien release from the solar company in order to close. This process can take weeks, and often months, so it’s not something you want to delay until closing is around the corner.

- Buyers Must Qualify for the Solar Loan
If your installed solar panels come with a loan payment, the buyer of your home has to qualify for that payment too. It doesn’t just transfer over automatically.

That means, in addition to qualifying for the home itself, the buyer must also qualify for the solar loan, which affects their debt-to-income ratio and overall loan approval. I’ve seen this scenario become a deal-breaker especially when interest rates are on the higher side.

- Removing Solar Panels Isn’t Simple
You might think, “If it becomes a problem, I’ll just remove the panels and take them with me.” Not so fast. Most solar companies do not allow that. I’ve had only one client successfully remove and reuse their panels, and that was the exception, not the rule.

You need to think of solar panels as a semi-permanent home improvement. They need an exit strategy, especially if you don’t plan on staying in your home forever.

## Proactive Steps That Can Save You Time and Money

Want to avoid these headaches down the line? Here’s what I suggest you do if you’re considering solar panels and might sell in the future:

- Pay for the system fully upfront, if at all possible.
- If financed, plan to pay it off through your home sale proceeds. Budget for this when evaluating your net.
- If you won’t be paying off the loan, work with your real estate professional in advance to: - Get all lien release paperwork started early - Understand what buyers would need to assume the loan - Have written authorization so your agent can coordinate directly with your solar and finance companies
Let your real estate professional help you. With your written authorization, I can handle a lot of the legwork to make the selling process smoother.
