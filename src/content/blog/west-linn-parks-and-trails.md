Is it a Saturday morning, and you're wondering where to go that isn't another store? Here's the honest answer: in West Linn, you're rarely more than a few minutes from a trail or a riverbank.

The city manages **more than 600 acres of park land** across about thirty sites. That's a lot for a town this size, and it's one of the things people mention most once they've lived here a while. Here are the ones worth knowing first.

## The big three

**Mary S. Young Park** is the one everyone ends up at. It's about **128 acres** along the Willamette, with roughly 5 to 8 miles of trails through the woods. There's a designated off-leash area for dogs, plus an off-leash beach area on the river, sports fields, a reservable shelter and restrooms. If you only have time for one park, start here.

**Willamette Park** sits where the Tualatin River meets the Willamette, a short walk from Historic Willamette Main Street. It's the town's river park: a boat ramp at Bernert Landing, a dock, two large shelters and a gazebo, a playground and wading pool, ball fields, and sand and grass volleyball courts.

**Wilderness Park** is **51.4 acres** of hiking trails, and it feels more like a forest than a city park. You can get in from Clark Street, Oregon City Boulevard, Prospect Street or Windsor Terrace, which makes it an easy walk from a lot of homes.

## Smaller places worth finding

Some of the best spots in town are the quieter ones:

- **Fields Bridge Park**, 19 acres on the Tualatin River, with community gardens, ball fields, basketball courts and paths.
- **Maddax Woods**, 7 acres with trails, picnic tables and access to the river.
- **Burnside Park**, about 10 acres on the Willamette with trails and good wildlife viewing.
- **Tanner Creek Park**, 10 acres with a skate park, a tennis court that doubles for pickleball, a water play area and a shelter.
- **Sunset Park**, 5.1 acres with basketball, tennis and two shelters.

The city's park list also includes river and nature sites like the Cedaroak Boat Ramp, Camassia Nature Preserve and Ibach Nature Park, which are lovely for a slower walk.

## Getting on the water

This is a river town, and it shows in the parks. Between the boat ramp at Willamette Park, the Cedaroak ramp, and the river frontage at Mary S. Young, Maddax Woods and Burnside, you've got more ways to reach the water than most people realize. The city also publishes a Willamette River Water Trail page if you paddle.

## How the parks fit into choosing a home

I'll be honest about the tradeoff. West Linn is hilly, and that means some neighborhoods sit right next to a trailhead while others are a short drive from the nearest big park. Neither is better. It just depends on how you want your week to feel.

If walking out your door onto a trail matters to you, it's worth looking at a map of the parks alongside a map of the homes for sale. It's a simple step before you start touring, and it can change which streets you fall for.
