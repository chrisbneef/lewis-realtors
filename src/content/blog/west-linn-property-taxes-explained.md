Property taxes are the line most buyers forget about until they are sitting at the closing table. In West Linn there are two things worth knowing early, because they surprise almost everyone: the rate is not what you would guess, and the bill is not based on what you paid for the house.

Here is how it actually works.

## The short answer

The effective property tax rate in West Linn is about **1.09 percent of a home's real market value**, and the median annual tax bill runs around **$7,668**. For a rough sense of scale, a home near the town's typical value lands in the low $7,000s a year in property tax.

That is a useful ballpark, but the word "effective" is hiding the part that trips people up, so let us open it up.

## Why West Linn's own city rate is low

People are often surprised to learn that the City of West Linn's permanent tax rate is only **$2.12 per $1,000 of assessed value**, described by the city as the lowest rate of any city in the surrounding area. Your city taxes are the small part of the bill. City services make up only about **13 percent** of what you pay.

The rest of your bill goes to the schools, Clackamas County, and voter-approved bond measures. So when a bill feels high, it is rarely the city line doing it. It is the schools and the bonds, which is the tradeoff West Linn buyers tend to make on purpose.

## The Measure 50 rule that trips buyers up

This is the big one. In Oregon, your property tax is calculated on your home's **assessed value**, not its market value or the price you just paid.

Under Measure 50, each property carries a Maximum Assessed Value that can only rise about **3 percent a year**, no matter how fast the market moves. Because West Linn home values have climbed far faster than 3 percent over the years, most homes now have an assessed value well below what they would sell for. Your tax is figured on the lower number.

The practical takeaway for a buyer: **do not estimate your tax by multiplying your purchase price by a rate.** That almost always overstates it. A longtime home down the street from a brand new one can have a much lower tax bill for the exact same market value, purely because its assessed value was locked in years ago.

A few things reset that locked-in value upward by more than 3 percent, and they are worth knowing before you buy:

- New construction, so a newly built home often carries a higher assessed value than an older neighbor.
- A major remodel or addition that adds real market value.
- Subdividing or partitioning the land.

## The caps behind all of it

West Linn's rates sit under Oregon's Measure 5 limits, which cap the tax on a home's real market value at **$10 per $1,000 for general government** and **$5 per $1,000 for schools**. Voter-approved bonds sit outside those caps, which is why a new school or library bond can nudge your bill up even when the base rates do not move.

## How to find your real number

The reliable way to know a West Linn home's actual tax is not a formula, it is the record. Clackamas County Assessment and Taxation publishes each property's assessed value and last year's bill. Before you write an offer, look up the specific home, not a percentage of the asking price.

## What this means when you buy or sell

If you are **buying**, budget from the property's real tax record, and remember that a newer or recently remodeled home may carry a higher assessed value, and a higher bill, than an older home at the same price. It is a small monthly difference that adds up over a mortgage.

If you are **selling**, expect buyers who have done their homework to ask about the tax history, especially if your home is newer. A clear, accurate number removes a common last-minute worry.
