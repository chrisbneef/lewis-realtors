Have you ever seen a little sign at the end of your street announcing a neighborhood meeting and thought, "Is that for me?" It is. And in West Linn, it's one of the best-kept secrets about how this town actually works.

## What a neighborhood association is here

West Linn has a formal neighborhood association program, written into city code. Each association covers a geographic area of town, and anyone who **lives, owns property or owns a business** inside its boundary can take part. Participation is voluntary and open to everyone inside the boundary.

The associations are independent volunteer groups, not city departments. They elect their own boards, set their own goals, and speak for their neighborhoods to the City Council, the Planning Commission and other city boards.

The city's May 2024 program guide lists **eleven** recognized associations, ranging from about 400 to 6,500 community members each. The city's current neighborhood page says **ten** are recognized today, so check the map for the latest. The names on the city map are:

## They have a real voice on land use

This is the part most people don't know until a project lands next door. Recognized associations get benefits that individual residents don't:

- They're **invited to pre-application conferences** for development planned in their neighborhood, before anything is formally filed.
- They can **testify at public hearings** with extra time that individuals don't get.
- Their **land use appeal fees are waived**.
- They receive information from the city on transportation, development and other issues affecting their area.
- They receive a **grant** from the city for neighborhood expenses.

Seven neighborhood plans have also been adopted into the city's Comprehensive Plan. Those plans describe what each area wants to keep and how it hopes to change over the next twenty years.

If you've followed the conversation around the Tannler Drive proposal this summer, you've seen how much these groups shape the public side of a land use decision.

## More than meetings

Associations also do the everyday work of a neighborhood. The city's guide mentions block parties, ivy pulls, cleanups, park upgrades and neighbors helping neighbors. Meetings are open to the public, held within city limits, and must offer a way to attend remotely, so you can listen in from your kitchen table.

Several associations have meetings coming up in mid-October, including Savanna Oaks, Robinwood, Parker Crest and Willamette. The city posts agendas and minutes on its neighborhood association pages.

## How to find yours

1. Open the city's neighborhood association map and find your address.
2. Visit that association's page on the city site for meeting dates and contact information.
3. If you're not sure, call West Linn City Hall at 503-657-0331. They'll point you in the right direction.

## Why I bring this up with buyers

When you buy a home, you're not just buying walls and a roof. You're joining a street, a set of neighbors, and a conversation about what happens around you next. Going to one association meeting before you buy is one of the easiest ways to learn what's coming to an area, from road projects to new development.

It's also a lovely way to meet people when you're new. You don't have to wait to be invited. If you'd like help figuring out which association covers a home you're considering, just ask. Reach out any time.
