Are you torn between West Linn and Lake Oswego, flipping between listings in both and wondering what's really different? You're not alone. It's one of the most searched questions about West Linn, and online the answers get buried under football scores from the two high schools' rivalry.

They're neighbors, they share a river, and from a distance they look alike. Up close, a few real differences matter when you own a home.

## Size and home values

Lake Oswego is the bigger city. Using the Census Bureau's American Community Survey (2024 five-year estimates):

| | West Linn | Lake Oswego |
|---|---|---|
| Population | 26,935 | 40,381 |
| Land area | 7.4 sq mi | 10.9 sq mi |
| Housing units | about 10,400 | about 18,100 |
| Median value of owner-occupied homes | $770,200 | $932,200 |

A note on that last row: those are owners' own estimates of their homes' value, gathered by survey over five years. They're useful for comparing the two cities, but they lag today's market. For what West Linn homes are **asking right now**, see our [market reports](/market-reports).

## City property tax rates

This one surprises people. Each city's own permanent tax rate per $1,000 of assessed value:

- **West Linn: $2.12**, which the city describes as the lowest among cities in the surrounding area.
- **Lake Oswego: about $4.97** for fiscal 2026-27 inside the Lake Oswego School District, plus a separate rate for the city's bonds.

That's only the city's slice. Your total bill also includes schools, the county and any voter-approved bonds, and it depends on your exact tax code area. In West Linn, the city's share is about 13 percent of a typical bill. I wrote more about how Oregon's system works in [What West Linn property taxes really cost](/blog/west-linn-property-taxes-explained). Always check a specific home's tax record before you budget.

## The lake question

Oswego Lake is beautiful, and it's private. The Lake Oswego Corporation manages it, and in its own words, "Access to an easement on Oswego Lake is a privilege, not a right."

Here's how it works:

- Access comes through **easements** tied to specific properties by historical recorded documents.
- Those rights **can't be bought, sold or transferred** separately. You have to live in the home that holds them.
- Members pay fees to both their easement association and the Lake Corporation. For 2026, the Lake Corporation lists $228 for swim and non-power use and $1,566 for a power boat.

If lake living is the dream, confirm a home's access in writing before you fall in love with it.

West Linn's water story is different. The Willamette and Tualatin rivers run along the city, and you reach them through public parks and boat ramps instead of private easements. I covered those in [West Linn parks and trails](/blog/west-linn-parks-and-trails).

## What downtown feels like

**Downtown Lake Oswego** is a lakefront business district along State Street, anchored by Lake View Village, a 100,000 square foot mixed-use center with restaurants and shops.

**West Linn** doesn't have one big downtown. Its closest thing is [Historic Willamette Main Street](/blog/historic-willamette-main-street), a small, walkable stretch of older storefronts that used to be its own town. Beyond that, shopping and dining spread across smaller commercial areas around town.

## Schools, by name only

The two cities are served by different districts. West Linn is in the **West Linn-Wilsonville School District**. Most of Lake Oswego is in the **Lake Oswego School District**. If schools are part of your decision, look up the specific attendance boundaries for any address you're considering, because they don't always follow city lines.

## So which one?

Honestly, it depends on what your daily life looks like. Lake Oswego is larger, with a busier downtown and a private lake. West Linn is smaller, hillier, more spread out, and built around its rivers and parks.
