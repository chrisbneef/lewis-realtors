"How much does a house in West Linn cost?" is the first question almost every buyer and seller asks me, and the honest answer is that it depends heavily on which part of town you are looking at. West Linn is small, but the spread from one neighborhood to the next is wide.

Here is where prices sit as of July 2026, using what is actually for sale right now.

## The one number, and why it needs context

Across West Linn, the median asking price for a single-family or multi-family home is about **$975,000**, with roughly 150 houses on the market and a typical listing sitting around 69 days before it sells or comes off. That is a measured market, not a frantic one.

Two things to keep in mind about that number:

- **These are asking prices, not sold prices.** Oregon is a non-disclosure state, which means closed sale prices are not public record. I pull real sold figures from RMLS for my clients, but the public numbers you see anywhere, including here, are what sellers are asking.
- **This counts houses, not everything.** I have left out condos, townhomes, and manufactured homes, because mixing them in pulls the median down and hides what a house actually costs. Bolton is a good example: fold in its riverfront condos and the median looks like the low $400s, which is not what a Bolton house sells for.

## Neighborhood by neighborhood

Grouped from the most attainable to the most exclusive, here is the median asking price for houses in each West Linn neighborhood right now.

**The entry into West Linn, roughly $700K to $800K.** Historic Bolton comes in lowest at about $700,000, which reflects its mix of Craftsman character homes and honest fixers. Parker Crest sits near $760,000, waterfront-adjacent Sunset around $785,000, and the former-campus pockets of Marylhurst near $800,000.

**The middle of the market, roughly $840K to $975K.** Hidden Springs runs about $839,000 and school-centric Rosemont Summit about $850,000. The walkable Willamette neighborhood, one of the most active in town with more than twenty houses listed, sits near $875,000. Newer Skyline Ridge reaches about $975,000.

**The luxury tier, past $1.2M.** Robinwood's larger and view properties push the median to about $1.22 million, and Savanna Oaks to roughly $1.28 million. Out on the rural edge, the acreage and estates of Stafford lead the market at about $2.35 million.

## What the spread tells you

If you are **buying**, the takeaway is that "West Linn" is not one price. The same budget that buys a character home in Bolton or Sunset is a stretch in Robinwood or Stafford, and the school feeder pattern, commute, and lot size change just as much as the price does. Start with the neighborhood, then the home.

If you are **selling**, that 69-day average is the number to watch. Homes that are priced to the real neighborhood evidence move; homes priced to a number from two years ago tend to sit and then chase the market down. Pricing off your specific block, not the citywide median, is where the value is.
